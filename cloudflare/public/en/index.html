ACCOUNT POOL / SWITCH WITHOUT LOGGING IN AGAIN v1.30.0

Another Codex account.
No second sign-in.

The right-click panel gains a "Codex accounts" section: the active account and every stashed backup at a glance, and clicking Switch performs an atomic rename swap — no login prompts, no QR codes.

  • Log in new

    Stash the current credentials into the pool and reopen ChatGPT for one sign-in; from then on the new account is one click away.

  • Never quits for you

    While ChatGPT/Codex runs, a switch parks itself until you quit, then completes within seconds; the running state is re-checked at the last moment before any file moves.

  • Quota follows account

    Every account remembers the last captured 5h and Weekly remaining percentages with reset times — check before you switch.

Manage the pool in place: rename, delete (to Trash), or import earlier hand-made backups with one click. API-key-mode auth files are never touched automatically.

03 / SHARED ACCOUNTS, COUNTED PER PERSON v1.23.0

One shared account.
Per-person numbers.

When several people share a Codex account, the quota becomes a black box: everyone sees what's left, nobody can say who spent it. Form a team and each Mac's local usage rolls up per member automatically, while account quotas are shared in real time — no more asking around.

  • Create

    Create a team in Settings → Usage & Team. Its management password is saved in Keychain; share only the invite code with members.

  • Invite

    Members join on their own Mac with the invite code and a name; the same name and passphrase later links another Mac.

  • Roll up

    Devices report local usage on their own; the app breaks it down by member, device and account, and usage and quota sharing can each be paused.

  • Team in the menu v1.31.0

    The left-click menu usage card switches to the team: whole team or a single member, current month and last 24 hours — no local Codex sign-in required.

Every member can click View team to open the read-only dashboard without entering a password. Managers use Manage team for direct access to invitations and devices. Team data stays isolated; quota history is kept for 90 days.

RIGHT-CLICK / THE OTHER HALF OF YOUR WORK

How do I get started?

Download the DMG and drag the app into Applications, then connect your provider account in Settings. The public build uses ad-hoc signing; if macOS says it can't verify the app, Control-click it and choose Open, or allow it in Privacy & Security.

How do I get the Windows edition?

The Windows edition is officially here with this update: zero-config GLM / MiniMax, auto-detected Codex, the Clash routing panel and bilingual text are all ready. The installer download channel opens soon — see the Windows section above for the feature list.

How is local usage different from account quota?

Account quota is the provider-side total; local usage comes from Codex logs on this Mac. Usage records are not the same as every underlying HTTP request, and costs are estimated from configured prices — not a bill.

Several of us share one Codex account — can we see who used what?

Create a team in Settings → Usage & Team; members join with the invite code and a name, and each person's local usage rolls up automatically. The full flow and the /team panel are covered in the shared-account section above; different teams cannot read each other's data.

Is my data uploaded automatically?

Codex local usage is computed on this Mac. Creating or joining a team enables usage reporting and quota sharing; pause each independently. Without a team, no team data is shared. Anonymous operational analytics have a separate switch. Everything on this page is sample data — your accounts, logs and app settings are never read.

When I switch Codex accounts, how is history attributed?

The new version prefers account information from the logs, falling back to consecutive local sign-in observations. Old records that can't be attributed reliably stay “Unknown” instead of being charged to the current account. Costs are estimated from configured prices — not a provider bill.
